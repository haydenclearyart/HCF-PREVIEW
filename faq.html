01

Getting started

What actually happens when I sign up?

We get on the phone. That's the first thing that happens — a proper call, me and you.

There's no form to fill out. No questionnaire, no PDF to email back, no dropdown menus. That stuff feels like paperwork, and paperwork is how coaching starts off cold.

On the call I ask about what you've done before, what stopped you last time, what your week actually looks like, what you've got to train with and where you're trying to get to. I ask a lot. You'll end up telling me things a form would never have thought to ask.

Then I go away and build your program off that conversation. Not off a checkbox.

How does online coaching work if you're not standing next to me?

Your program lives on your phone, in an app called Trainerize. It's live — not a PDF that goes stale the second something changes.

Every exercise has a demo video, so you're never standing there guessing what a Romanian deadlift is meant to look like. You log your sets as you go, and it shows you last time's numbers right there on the screen. You always know exactly what you're chasing.

You check in with me every week. Between check-ins, you message me on whatever you actually use — DM, text, WhatsApp, whatever's easiest for you. I'm not making you download something new just to ask a question.

If something feels wrong mid-session, ask me then. Don't sit on it for a week.

Do I need a gym?

No. The program gets built around what you've actually got.

Full gym, a couple of dumbbells in the garage, or nothing but the floor — I'll write for what's in front of you. You tell me on the call and that's what you get.

Same goes if that changes halfway through. Gym membership runs out, you go away for work, you move house — tell me and the program moves with you.

How much time do I need each week?

Tell me what you've realistically got and I'll build to it. Three sessions a week is plenty to get somewhere real. Two is enough to start.

I'd rather write you three sessions you'll actually do than five you'll skip two of and feel guilty about.

The program that fits your week beats the perfect program every single time.

02

Is this for me

I've started and stopped before. Why would this be any different?

Because that's exactly who I built this for.

Most coaching assumes you're already going and just need a better plan. If that were true, you wouldn't have stopped four times already. The plan was never the problem.

What's different here is that when it goes sideways — and it will — you tell me, and we adjust. You don't go quiet for three weeks and then come back embarrassed. Everyone can do week one. This is built for week seven.

I'm a complete beginner. Am I going to be out of my depth?

No. Beginners are honestly the easiest people to coach, because there's nothing to undo.

You'll get shown every movement, you'll start lighter than you think you should, and you'll be told why we're doing it that way.

And on the thing you're actually worried about — nobody in there is watching you. They're all looking at themselves in the mirror.

I don't want to get huge or shredded. I just want to feel better. Is this still for me?

Yes. Honestly, that's most of the people I work with.

"Feel better" is a real goal. More energy, clothes fitting properly, getting up off the floor without making a noise, sleeping through the night. You don't need a stage tan to justify training.

Tell me what better looks like for you, and that's what we build toward.

03

What you get

What's actually included?

Every package gets all of it:

  • A training program built around your goal, your schedule and your equipment
  • Nutrition guidance that works in a real week
  • Weekly check-ins
  • Direct access to me — not a support team, not a template reply
  • All five Fit Files guides
  • Program adjustments whenever life changes
Why does every package include the same thing?

Because I'm not going to hold your nutrition hostage to upsell you into a bigger tier.

You're not paying for more features. You're paying for more time. Four weeks teaches you. Eight weeks lets you practise it when things go wrong. Twelve weeks makes it normal.

The resources are identical. The runway is what changes.

Which package should I pick?

Honest answer: the longest one you can commit to without resenting it.

Four weeks will show you what this looks like. Eight is where most people actually change something. Twelve is where it stops feeling like a program and starts just being how you live.

If you're still not sure, start with The Trust. It's free, it's nine days, and you'll know by the end of it whether the way I coach suits you.

04

Money

Can I pay weekly instead of all at once?

Yes. Weekly direct debit, or pay upfront and save up to 20%.

Upfront is cheaper. Weekly is easier on the bank account. Neither one gets you a different program.

What if I need to pause, or something happens and I have to stop?

Talk to me.

I'm not going to write a policy here, because every situation is different and a policy is just me guessing at yours in advance. People get injured. People lose jobs. People have things happen that have nothing at all to do with training.

If something comes up, tell me and we'll work it out between us. I'd rather sort it out properly with you than hide behind terms and conditions.

05

Training & food

What if I've got an injury or something medical going on?

Tell me on the call. All of it.

I'm a coach, not a physio and not a doctor. If something needs proper medical attention I'll say so and send you to someone who's qualified for it — that's not me dodging, it's just the honest line.

If you've been cleared to train, I'll write around it. Working around a dodgy shoulder or a cranky lower back is completely normal. Hiding it from me until week three is what causes the problems.

Do I get a meal plan?

No, and that's on purpose.

Meal plans work until Thursday, when there's a birthday dinner or you've run out of chicken. Then they fall apart and you feel like you failed. You didn't. The plan was rubbish.

What you get instead is a way of eating that survives a real week — what to prioritise, how to build a meal, how to handle the pub without writing off the whole thing. You'll actually understand it, which means it still works when I'm not around.

Do I have to weigh my food and track macros?

Not if you don't want to.

Tracking is a tool. It's genuinely useful for some people and miserable for others — and the miserable ones quit.

If it works for you, great, I'll show you how to do it properly. If the thought of weighing your breakfast makes you want to give up before you start, we'll do it a different way. There's more than one road here.

06

The finish line

How do I know it's actually working?

Two ways, and you'll see both.

The obvious one is in the app. Every session shows you what you lifted last time, so you're always training against your own numbers. When the weight you were fighting for in week two is your warm-up in week seven, that's not a feeling — that's on the screen in front of you.

The other one is the stuff numbers don't catch. Sleeping properly. Stairs not being an event. Getting through a week that would have derailed you six months ago. We talk about that in check-ins deliberately, because that's usually the change that actually sticks.

What happens at the end?

You leave. And ideally, you don't come back.

That's the actual goal. If you finish twelve weeks and still need me to tell you what to do on a Tuesday, I've done a bad job.

You should walk away able to write your own week, adjust it when life shifts, and eat without checking in with me first.

Hire me to fire me. I mean it.
